# THE THREE PILLARS (above every other rule)
Procrastination, fear of overwhelm, flexibility. Every feature, every tool, every decision, every word exists for these. Gate for every round and every change: write one line "serves pillar: X because Y". If it serves none, or works against one, do not build it.

# MOTTO
**A low-dopamine, easily distracted mind that wants discipline.** The app builds discipline for exactly this mind, and the three pillars are how.
- Do not rely on motivation, novelty or dopamine spikes: no loud rewards, confetti, noisy streaks or punishing counters. Rewards are quiet and honest (a round done, a clear "done" state, calm progress).
- Reduce friction to start and remove distraction: one thing in sight, tiny first step, cues and routines (War is the ritual), few choices, silent defaults.
- Discipline = repeatable small actions, not intensity: protect consistency, make recovery after a bad day free (flexibility), never shame.
- Procrastination = tiny start and clear cue; overwhelm = hide the mountain; flexibility = nothing locked so a missed day never breaks the system.

# FIRM, NOT SOFT (user: "never be light with me")
Calm is not light. Discipline needs honesty and standards. The pillars reduce friction and overwhelm, they never lower the bar.
- Keep the intention of the work visible: the work target (portfolio and client work, 8 pomodoros a day), Gate dates, and the weekly goals stay on screen as facts. Never hide them to feel better.
- Tell the truth in neutral words: plan vs actual, how many tasks carried over, how many days a work block was missed. Facts, no shame, no flattery.
- Missing is recorded, not erased: rollover keeps a visible history, and the weekly review shows the pattern.
- Work that serves the intention (portfolio, client, learning) is protected first when time is short; other stuff yields first.
- Repeated postponing (3+) requires a decision: do it now, shrink it, split it or drop it. Postponing forever is not an option.
- Firm tone, short and direct. No cheerleading, no soft fluff.

# THE SEASON (user, 13 Nov)
This is a hard, deliberate season of 3 to 4 years, especially this year: heavy work plus daily learning, accepted as suffering that is not permanent. The app holds the line during the season and protects recovery so it can be sustained.

# FRAMING: SELF AS A COMPANY (user definition, 13 Nov)
Ahmed runs himself as a company.
- **Work = design**: client design work AND portfolio projects (his personal design projects, e.g. the HASM showcase, which is a portfolio project and never a brand item). It is a daily employee-style commitment: 8 to 10 hours a day, every working day. The daily work target is a setting (8 to 10 h), shown as a plain fact. Only design work counts toward it.
- **Learning is a daily protected block**, not optional and not counted as work hours. A work-only day is not a complete day: every day has the work block plus a learning block (default minimum in settings). It is hard, and it is a season, not permanent: the next 3 to 4 years, especially this year, to build something.
- **Other stuff = everything else that is not work**: branding, marketing (LinkedIn), R&D, health, money, admin, home. It is vital and generates revenue and growth, but it is outside the work clock: it never counts toward the work hours and never replaces them.
- Departments = these two groups, each with buckets (projects), weekly targets and a plan vs actual. Weekly planning is the management meeting; Gates are company milestones.
- Executive tone: short, factual, decision-oriented. Targets, actuals, variances.
- When time is short: work hours are protected first; other stuff keeps minimums (rest, health), not zero.

# STAKES ARE REAL (user, 13 Nov)
Missed work means late revenue, and late revenue puts family obligations at risk. Work hours are the top priority of the whole app, above every other feature and every comfort.
- When anything conflicts with work hours, work wins. No feature, round or screen may take time, attention or focus away from design work.
- The daily work block is protected: nothing else is offered or scheduled inside it, and War mode stays the way into it.
- Be real: show work hours done vs target and client deadlines as plain facts, early (not at the last day), so a shortfall is seen while it can still be fixed.
- Plans must be holdable: a realistic 8 to 10 h day with buffer, not a heroic one that breaks by Wednesday. Consistency beats intensity.
- Other stuff keeps only its protected minimums; it never eats work hours.

# BEAT YOURSELF (user, 13 Nov)
The ethos is to push hard and work as much as possible to build something. The app serves that, as training does: high standards, structured stretch, protected recovery.
- Two levels: a baseline (the daily work target, 8 h) that is the standard, and a stretch (up to 10 h or more) that is always one tap away and tracked. Exceeding the target is recorded and celebrated quietly as a personal best, never required, never allowed to hide a missed baseline.
- The opponent is yesterday's self: show best day, best week and the trend of work hours against his own past, as plain numbers.
- Pushing hard is sustained by recovery, so recovery is part of the system, not a weakness: sleep minimum, the Friday rest day and buffer days stay protected, and work hours that cost sleep are flagged as facts.
- Raise the bar gradually and on purpose (the stretch target can be raised in settings); never lower it silently.

# FOCUS, DEEP WORK AND CHUNKING (user, 13 Nov)
- Focus work and deep work are the core of the work block: long uninterrupted blocks on one design task (the 50/10 rhythm), no notifications, one thing in sight.
- Chunking: allocate similar things together so they get finished. Group similar tasks into the same block or day (one design project per deep block; all small admin, replies and LinkedIn comments in one shallow batch; learning in its own block), and keep a bucket's tasks together instead of scattering them.
- Planning, refill, rollover and rescheduling must respect chunks: re-flow moves a whole chunk, not single tasks apart from their group; a new task joins the chunk of its bucket or type by default; deep blocks never get shallow tasks mixed in.
- Show the day as a few chunks (work chunks, learning chunk, shallow batch), not as a long list of tasks. That also lowers cognitive load.

# VISUAL TASTE (user is a senior designer; applies to every screen)
- Minimal text and minimal typography so the eye can focus: few type sizes (one body, one small, one title), light weights, no bold walls, no ALL CAPS shouting, no decoration.
- Light text on calm surfaces; clear, calm, quiet. Never loud colour; colour only as a small signal.
- Generous white space (air) between and inside groups. Use few rectangles: group by chunk and separate chunks with thin hairline lines (the style already in the app and liked by the user), not by stacking boxes. Space plus hairlines do the grouping.
- Small tabs under the text: the category or type shown as a tiny, light tag/label under the task title (work, learning, shallow, bucket, due). They are tiny on purpose, they save time and carry the categorisation. Keep them consistent in size, weight and position everywhere.
- Designer-grade alignment: one spacing scale (multiples of 4 or 8 px), one left edge per column, consistent padding and radius, baseline-aligned text, aligned numbers and time columns, consistent row heights inside a list, optical alignment of icons and text. Check alignment and white space at 390 and 1300 on every change.
- Buckets and cards may differ in size by content, but their edges, gutters and inner padding stay on the grid.

# UX LOGIC: REPEATED ACTIONS GO UP, NOT DOWN THE LIST (user, 14 Nov)
- If an action repeats on every row (for example Detach under each task in a chunk or parent), do not repeat the button. Put it once in the header and use selection: select the tasks (tap or hold to enter select mode, checkboxes appear), then one action bar acts on the selection: Detach, Group, Move, Reschedule, Archive.
- The same rule applies everywhere: one action, one place; rows stay clean text plus a tiny tag.

# ONE PROJECT AT A TIME; CLIENT PROJECTS RUN BESIDE PORTFOLIO (user, 14 Nov)
- He works on one project at a time (one project per deep block). All current projects (HASM, Javelin, Mugassam and the rest) are PORTFOLIO projects, so exactly one portfolio project is active at a time; the next ones wait in an ordered "Up next" queue on the Later shelf.
- The only exception is a CLIENT project, which runs in parallel with the active portfolio project. A client project can land at any time: he creates its own bucket and marks it as Client.
- Client buckets are distinctive (a tiny quiet "Client" tag under the name, its own place on Today and in the waterfall), have a deadline and a delivery date, and follow the shared schedule: client work gets the first deep block (revenue and stakes come first), the active portfolio project keeps the remaining work blocks. If a client project lands, the portfolio project shrinks to the remaining blocks and resumes full blocks when the client project is delivered.
- The bucket has a type: Portfolio (default) or Client. Creating or editing a bucket asks for the type in one tap. Starting a second portfolio project asks once: "One portfolio project at a time: pause X?" with Undo.
- Both client and portfolio count as WORK (design), with client first when time is short.

# REORDERING IS ALWAYS A GESTURE (user, 14 Nov)
Never offer "Move up" or "Move down" as a button or menu item, anywhere in the app, now or in future features. Any list that has an order (tasks, subtasks, rows in a chunk, buckets, the Up next queue, inbox, waterfall rows, steps, habits, music queue, gym lists and so on) is reordered by HOLD, then DRAG up or down, with a clear lift state (slightly raised row, hairline drop indicator), auto-scroll at the edges, and Undo. On desktop a quiet grip appears on hover; no permanent grip icons on touch. Pair with the other gestures: swipe right done, swipe left reschedule, hold-drag reorder, tap opens.

# Design rules (user standing order, 13 Nov)
Every task and feature is judged by how Ahmed will actually use it. Applies to architect, builder and runner.

0. **Scope: do what was asked, nothing more.** If there is a better way to do something, write it as a short suggestion for Ahmed to accept or ignore. Never build or apply it unasked. This applies to the planner too: plans contain only what he requested, with ideas in a separate "Suggestions" list at the end.
1. **Journey first.** Before building, write a 5-line "day in use" projection: when he opens it, what he wants, the fewest taps, what he sees after. Put it at the top of each plan round. If the journey is not simple, redesign before coding.
2. **Whole feature, not half.** A feature ships with all its logic: add, edit, move, undo, delete or archive, empty state, and how it shows everywhere it appears (Today, Plan, Review, projects, buckets).
3. **Simple and calm.** Show the next 1 to 3 things, collapse the rest. Counts are for projects and milestones, never tiny steps. Defaults are sensible so he types as little as possible.
4. **Editable by him.** Everything the app or Claude creates (names, dates, buckets, sprints) can be changed in the app without help.
5. **No surprises.** Every change has Undo or a rollback. Data changes get a backup first.
6. **Seamless.** Same gestures and words in every place. Calm glass style, no new icons, no em dashes.
7. **Test the journey.** Each round has a Playwright check that follows the projected journey at 390 and 1300.
8. **Gestures over buttons.** Use swipe and long-press to replace buttons and cut clutter. Keep one gesture vocabulary everywhere (same as today: swipe right = done, swipe left = reschedule; long-press = quick menu with edit, move, rename, archive, delete; drag = move between days or buckets). Every gesture also has a visible fallback (a tap opens the same menu) and a one-time subtle hint, never a permanent extra button. Undo after every gesture.
9. **Brain-friendly design (neuroscience and cognitive load).** Treat attention and working memory as scarce.
   - Working memory holds about 4 chunks: show at most 3 to 4 items per group; collapse the rest behind one "more".
   - One primary action per screen and per card; one decision at a time; good defaults so he decides less (decision fatigue).
   - Recognition over recall: show the thing, do not make him remember where it is. Keep layouts and positions stable so spatial memory works.
   - Progressive disclosure: detail only on tap or long-press. Chunk related items into labelled groups.
   - Protect focus: avoid context switches, pop-ups and motion; calm colour, high contrast text, no flashing; no more than one notification at a time.
   - Show clear progress and a visible "done" state (closure reduces mental load); ask for few inputs and let him capture first, organise later.
   - Every new feature includes a short "cognitive load check" line in its round: items on screen, decisions asked, taps to finish.
10. **User pillars: procrastination, fear of overwhelm, flexibility seeker.** Every round must serve these three.
   - Procrastination: make starting tiny. Every task offers a "just 5 minutes" start (timer or first step), the next action is always one tap away, capture takes one line, and nothing demands a decision before he can begin.
   - Fear of overwhelm: never show the whole mountain. Default views show today's one to three things and a calm count; backlog and the rest stay collapsed; big tasks open into a small first step; no red overdue walls, no guilt language, no streak punishment.
   - Flexibility: nothing is locked. Move, shrink, split, postpone or drop any task or bucket in one gesture with Undo; buffer days and soft dates (a date is a plan, not a verdict); "not today" is a normal, free action; plans re-flow easily when the day changes.
   - Wording is gentle and neutral ("moved to Saturday", not "missed").
